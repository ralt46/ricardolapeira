Project: Building the User Experience of an AI Chatbot

Note: due to a non-disclosure agreement (NDA), I cannot share many details about this project. If you would like to learn more, please get in touch directly with me.

Most companies want to benefit from the power of AI. They understand that there are many ways to create solutions that deliver real value. The challenge that many don't undersand is that, when it comes to implementing AI, the quality of the content and user experience also matter a lot.

Everyday chatbots are no exception. An AI chatbot can be technically impressive, but it can fail to deliver if it is not able to operate according to user expectations. It is essential that the team working on the product understands this to build something that talks directly to users.

Read on to find out how I crafted the overall user experience of an AI chatbot for a US-based NGO that wanted to use its product across multiple locations in Latin America.

Context

I was brought in to be part of a team for a custom built AI chatbot. My role involved aligning the product, specifically its content, with the user's expectations, or in other words, working on the alignment. The tech stack had already been defined when I joined, but the entire user experience was yet to be designed. Although my role was officially that of a UX Writer, I was responsible for doing user research as well. Having done this in the past, I designed a complete work sprint to interview users, prototype and test the initial version of the chatbot, all while aiming to get the voice and tone right. This involved designing interviews, arranging a research agenda, building user mental models, and synthesizing the data into easily understandable insights that the team could work with.

From the first moment we started doing research we noticed a couple of things. First, most users, no matter where they were located, seemed to have similar expectations. They had very clear ideas on what they expected the tool to do depending on the task at hand. This validated the idea that the chatbot should achieve specific outcomes and at the same time made our work easier, as it meant we could deliver a single version for multiple locations. The second aspect we started noticing was unfortunately that the chosen tech stack was somewhat problematic. It didn't really meet the expectations in terms of output quality and it was incompatible with other technologies like WhatsApp, a critical requirement, so we flagged it as a potential threat.

Problem

Most digital products focus exclusively on technical aspects. If there is time and budget left, other aspects are then considered. Luckily, this wasn't the case of this project. From the first moment the user experience had been a fundamental part. This helped guarantee that the users well fully considered and their inputs integrated into the product. Because of this, our team had a clear idea of what the chatbot had to do.

That is why, when things weren't really working as expected, we immediately identified that something was not right. After some tests, we came to the conclusion that the problem was the tech stack. Although it had been selected for its ease of use, we noticed that this came with a cost: there was little room for customizing it in the way we needed to. This was problematic because it meant reconsidering how the product had been built, and time was not on our side. The first version had to be delivered soon and a pivot meant a lot of rework.

Solution

After considering different alternatives, we ended up choosing the not-so-easy one. As a team, we decided that the best we could was to redesign the technical part of the product using a different tech stack. This meant rebuilding some components rather than building it all from scratch. Because we had been very methodic in our work, we were easily able to extract what was already working and use it for the alternative version. Having done our user research, we also had a great deal of information that helped us shape the behavior of this new version.

Given the project timelines, we knew that this solution needed to be both fast and effective. Indeed, the new version was up and running within the given deadlines, and it was thoroughly tested with great results.

Proof of the success of the product was that the client ended up signing an additional contract for the next iteration of the product. They were really excited to see their idea taking shape and, given the positive results throughout the project, they knew there was much more to work on. It helped that their users gave them very positive feedback on the product, reaffirming us that we made the right decision changing the tech stack.

Insights

This wasn't a difficult project, but it could have easily gone astray if it wasn't for the rigorous user research done. This helped align the product with user expectations, but most importantly, it helped us realize that the initial tech stack was not the correct one.

The easy decision would have been to leave everything as it was. Being very honest, the client would have probably never noticed. However, that would have ended up significantly compromising the quality of the entire project and its potential impact. This decision did not represent our work ethic. In the end, we made the right call to change the tech stack.

AI chatbots are a still somewhat experimental technology where tools are constantly and quickly evolving. Many organizations want to implement them but they don't understand how to do it. Here are some key insights learned in the process that any organization should keep in mind to successfully implement an AI chatbot:

  • Developing the chatbot is important, but so is understanding your users. Make sure to give both aspects the importance they deserve.
  • Decisions like changing the tech stack when the project has already started are not easy. However, they can save your organization lots of headaches in the future.
  • When building an AI chatbot, getting the user experience right isn't just a nice-to-have. They will not be impressed by how your chatbot performs but whether it delivers what they need.
  • Being transparent to your clients isn't just the right thing to do. It is also the smart thing to do and will most likely always pay off in the long term.
